Mathematical formulation of the coupling demonstration

Equations, derivations and numerical checks for the toy model of a gene circuit coupled to a metabolic network. Companion to the demonstration report, in the same notation as the code.

Scope. These are exact statements about the toy model, whose network and parameters are invented. They show the structure of the method. They are not claims about real red cells.

What this page establishes

  • The network side is a linear program with an exact answer. The set of feasible effector fluxes is an interval [0,V] (Lemma 1), so the cell delivers exactly min{κ,V} whatever the circuit asks for. For this network V has a closed form (Proposition 1).
  • The circuit side is three ODEs that meet the network only through that flux map.
  • In the mature stage a quasi steady state formula predicts the toxin level, and it implies an upper bound on the protected life of any circuit design (Propositions 2 and 3).
  • Expression strength and kcat are structurally non identifiable from toxin data, because they enter only as a product (Proposition 4).
  • Every formula is recomputed against a linear program or against the demo's own output in section 10.

2. The network

2.1 Reactions and balances

Twelve reactions. With atp/adp and nadph/nadp as conserved pairs, ten metabolites give eight independent balances. Writing δ=1 for an ATP using effector and δ=0 for an NADPH using effector, the steady state Sv=0 reads

vex,glc−vHK=0(glc)vHK−vGLYC−vPPP=0(g6p)2vGLYC+53vPPP−vLDH=0(pyr)vLDH−vex,lac=0(lac)−vHK+3vGLYC+83vPPP−vATPase−vexpr−δvDET=0(atp)2vPPP−vGSH−(1−δ)vDET=0(nadph)vex,x−vDET=0(toxin)vDET−vex,y=0(product)
(1)

Two consequences are used throughout. Eliminating vGLYC with the hexokinase flux f=vHK and the PPP flux p=vPPP gives

vGLYC=f−p≥0,vGSH+(1−δ)vDET=2p,2f−13p=vATPase+vexpr+δvDET.
(2)

That is, NADPH is made only by the PPP, and ATP is made by glycolysis at two per glucose minus a third of the PPP flux. The full matrix is in the box below.

3. Level 1: what the network can deliver

3.1 Network capacity and delivered flux

The network capacity is the largest effector flux the cell can support while meeting maintenance (the maximum that flux variability analysis returns for that reaction), and the delivered flux is the largest one when the circuit offers at most b:

V(a,c)=max{vDET: v∈Π(a,c,u¯)},v⋆(a,c,b)=max{vDET: v∈Π(a,c,b)}.
(4)
Lemma 1 (interval). Let c≤cmax(a), so that Π(a,c,u¯)≠∅. Then the set of feasible effector fluxes is the interval [0,V(a,c)], and
v⋆(a,c,b)=min{b, V(a,c)}.
(5)
If c>cmax(a) the problem is infeasible (an energy crisis).
Proof. Π(a,c,u¯) is a convex polytope, and its image under the coordinate map v↦vDET is a convex subset of the line, hence an interval. It contains 0, because the maintenance solution with vex,x=vDET=vex,y=0 is feasible exactly when Π≠∅. Its upper end is V by definition. If b≥V the bound b is not active; if b<V then b lies in the interval and is attained. ◻

This is what lets the demo replace a linear program at every time step by a table: the network capacity depends only on (a,c), and the circuit enters through min{b,V}.

3.2 Closed forms

Proposition 1 (network capacity). Let H=H(a), g=g(a). For the NADPH using effector,
V(a,c)=2min{g, H, 3(2H−mA−c)}−mN,feasible⟺c≤cmax(a)=2H(a)−mA−16mN.
(6)
For the ATP using effector,
VATP(a,c)=2H(a)−16mN−mA−c,feasible⟺c≤cmax(a).
(7)
Proof (NADPH effector). By (2), vDET=2p−vGSH≤2p−mN, so V is largest for the largest feasible p. The constraints on p are p≤g (G6PD capacity), p≤f≤H (the PPP is fed by hexokinase), and the ATP balance 2f−13p≥mA+c. The left side increases with f, so take f=H; the ATP balance becomes p≤3(2H−mA−c). Hence p≤p¯:=min{g,H,3(2H−mA−c)} and V=2p¯−mN. Feasibility needs p¯≥mN/2 (so that vGSH≥mN is possible); the binding term is the third, which gives c≤2H−mA−mN/6. ATP effector. Now vGSH=2p≥mN, so the smallest p=mN/2 is best, and the ATP balance gives vDET=2H−13p−mA−c with p=mN/2. ◻

In the demo's parameters the first branch of the minimum, g(a), is the active one for every age from 0 to 120 d when c=0 (the mature stage), and at age 0 for every cost up to 1.0667, which is 2H−mA−g0/3 (the expression stage). There V=2g(a)−mN, which is 0.68 for a young cell, and the network capacity for the ATP effector is 1.18 at age 0. Table interpolation in the code is exact up to the kinks of this piecewise linear function.

3.3 Marginal values (what one more unit of a resource buys)

3.4 Redox reserve and the operating window

Fix the effector at flux v≤V and ask how far the basal redox flux could be raised. Since vGSH=2p−v and p≤p¯ whatever v is,

Λ(a,c;v)=max{vGSH: v∈Π, vDET=v}mN=2p¯−vmN=1+V(a,c)−vmN.
(8)

With the offered bound b the delivered flux is v=min{b,V}. The effector is useful if v≥η and safe if Λ≥ρ. If b>V the reserve is 1<ρ, so only b≤V qualifies, and then V−b≥(ρ−1)mN. Therefore

W(a)=[η,  V(a)−(ρ−1)mN],W(a)≠∅⟺V(a)≥Θ=η+(ρ−1)mN.
(9)

With V=2g−mN the window closes where g(a*)=(Θ+mN)/2, and the effector cannot even reach η after aη where g(aη)=(η+mN)/2:

a*=τGlog22g0Θ+mN=57.6 d,aη=τGlog22g0η+mN=107.7 d.
(10)

4. Data and constraining the network

4.1 How the synthetic data are generated

For a true value θi with coefficient of variation CV, replicate j is lognormal with mean θi:

yij=θiexp(σξij−12σ2),ξij~N(0,1),σ2=ln(1+CV2),
(11)

with n=4, CV=6% for exchange rates (D1), n=4, 15% for the tracer fraction (D2), n=3, 10% for enzyme activity (D3), n=3, 12% for the toxin level (D4) and n=3, 15% for the enzyme amount (D5). The 95% interval used as a flux bound is the t interval y¯±t0.975,n−1s/n.

The true baseline fluxes (no circuit) are the parsimonious solution min1⊤v subject to v∈Π(a,0,0). With no effector, NADPH is used only by the basal reaction, so the minimal solution has

vPPP=mN2,vHK=mA+mN/62=0.41,vex,lac=2(vHK−vPPP)+53vPPP=0.80,
(12)

and a PPP share ϕ=vPPP/vHK=0.146, independent of age while hexokinase is not limiting. This is why the exchange data in the demo are flat.

4.2 Constraining and sampling the flux space

The exchange intervals [g―,g¯] and [ℓ―,ℓ¯] for glucose and lactate become bounds on vex,glc and vex,lac. The tracer interval [ϕ―,ϕ¯] for the PPP share is a pair of linear inequalities, because ϕ=vPPP/vHK and vHK≥0:

vPPP−ϕ¯vHK≤0,−vPPP+ϕ―vHK≤0.
(13)

Rather than optimise, the demo samples the polytope Ω={v: Sv=0, ℓ≤v≤u, Av≤0} by hit and run. Let x be the free coordinates (those with uj>ℓj), Sf the corresponding columns and Z an orthonormal basis of kerSf. From a point x∈Ω draw w~N(0,I), set d=Zw/‖Zw‖ and move to x+td with t uniform on

[tlo,thi]=[maxi:di≠0min{ℓi−xidi,ui−xidi}, mini:di≠0max{ℓi−xidi,ui−xidi}] ∩ {t: A(x+td)≤0}.
(14)

The chain has the uniform distribution on Ω as its stationary law, so it describes the feasible flux states without choosing an objective. The starting point is the mean of random vertices (an interior point), with 500 burn in steps and a thinning of 20. Flux variability analysis is the companion problem min/maxvj over Ω.

4.3 Enzyme activity and the predicted network capacity

Activities relative to young cells follow log2Aij=β0+β1ai+eij. Least squares gives

β^=(X⊤X)−1X⊤y,Σ^=s2(X⊤X)−1,τ^=−1/β^1.
(15)

Draws β(s)~N(β^,Σ^) give G6PD capacity curves g(s)(a)=g02β0(s)+β1(s)a and, through Proposition 1, network capacity curves V(g(s)(a),a) whose 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles form the band in the demo. Without the enzyme assay the same band is computed from a prior on (g0,τG) that is log uniform on [0.15,1]×[20,200] d and conditioned on g(120)≥mN/2 (capacity must cover the basal redox need).

5. The circuit model

With the stage indicator χ(t)=1 for t<Texp and 0 afterwards, and h(u;K)=u2/(K2+u2):

dRdt=χ(t)aRh(X;KX)−dRR,dEdt=χ(t)aEh(R;KR)−δ(t)E,dXdt=L−vDET−kcX.
(16)

Here δ(t)=δA for t<Texp and δM afterwards. Expression costs ATP, drawn only in the expression stage, and the enzyme can carry at most the kinetic bound:

c(t)=χ(t)[cRaRh(X;KX)+cEaEh(R;KR)],κ(t)=kEXKm+X.
(17)

Expression stage. Setting the derivatives to zero with χ=1 and vDET=κ (the network is not limiting) gives a fixed point X* that solves one scalar equation:

κ(E*(X*),X*)+kcX*=L,R*(X)=aRdRh(X;KX),E*(X)=aEδAh(R*(X);KR).
(18)

This is the self regulation of the sensor: more toxin raises R and then E, which removes toxin. For the true design X*=1.63 at an expression cost of 0.16. Held on for 40 days the ODE converges to it (relative difference 5e-09). The six day stage in the demo ends at 1.85, still on the way, because the approach is a damped oscillation.

Mature stage. With χ=0 the regulator decays, R(t)=R(Texp)e−dR(t−Texp), within about two days, and the effector follows E(a)=E02−a/hE with E0=0.260 for the true design. No new protein is made, so the circuit is open loop.

6. The coupling

At each time t the circuit hands the network a bound b=κ(t) and an expression cost c(t), and the network returns the delivered flux. By Lemma 1

vDET(t)={min{κ(t), V(a(t),c(t))}c(t)≤cmax0  (crisis, flagged)c(t)>cmax.
(19)

This closes the loop. The ODEs set κ and c, and the network sets vDET, which drives the toxin level that the sensor reads. The scheme is operator splitting with forward Euler, Δt=0.5 h, and a guard that stops the toxin level going negative:

vn←min{vn, max{Xn/Δt+L−kcXn,0}},Xn+1=max{Xn+Δt(L−vn−kcXn), 10−6}.
(20)

R and E are advanced with the same step. The demo solves the linear program at every step for the true design and uses a table of V for the design search; Lemma 1 says the two are the same map, and section 10 confirms it.

7. Quasi steady state of the mature stage

The toxin relaxes on a time scale of at most 1/kc=50 h, while the enzyme and the network capacity change over days to months. Setting dX/dt=0 at fixed age gives L=min{κ(E,X),V}+kcX. Both Fκ(X)=κ+kcX and FV(X)=V+kcX are strictly increasing in X, so min{Fκ,FV}=L has a unique root, and it is the larger of the two single roots.

Proposition 2 (quasi steady state).
Xqs(a)=max{Xκ(a), XV(a)},XV=L−V(a)kc,Xκ=B+B2+4kcLKm2kc,B=L−kcKm−kE(a).
(21)
Consequently the toxin is at or below target if and only if the enzyme and the network can each deliver the needed flux at the target level:
Xqs≤X¯⟺κ(E,X¯)≥η  and  V(a)≥η.
(22)
Proof. Xκ solves (L−kcX)(Km+X)=kEX, that is kcX2−BX−LKm=0, whose positive root is the formula. The equivalence follows from min{Fκ,FV}(X¯)≥L and monotonicity. ◻

The first condition fails at aκ=hElog2E0kX¯η(Km+X¯)=118.9 d, the second at aη=107.7 d, so the toxin is lost at atox=min{aκ,aη}=107.7 d. The network, not the enzyme, is the first to fail. The simulation gives 110 d on daily records. The reserve in the quasi steady state is Λ=1+(V−(L−kcXqs))/mN, which crosses ρ at 51.9 d (simulation: 52 d).

8. An upper bound on every design

A design is protected at age a if the toxin is at target and the redox reserve is intact. The protected fraction of the 120 days of mature life is

P(θ)=1120∑a=01191[X(a)≤X¯  and  Λ(a)≥ρ].
(23)
Proposition 3 (upper bound on design performance). In the quasi steady state, protection at age a implies V(a)≥Θ. Hence for every design θ
P(θ) ≤ P¯=1120|{a∈{0,…,119}: V(a)≥Θ}|,
(24)
and P¯ depends only on the network and the G6PD decline, not on the circuit.
Proof. Protection gives Xqs≤X¯, so the delivered flux is v=L−kcXqs≥L−kcX¯=η. The reserve condition 1+(V−v)/mN≥ρ then gives V≥v+(ρ−1)mN≥η+(ρ−1)mN=Θ. ◻

With V=2g−mN the set {a:V(a)≥Θ} is {0,…,⌊a*⌋}, so P¯=(⌊a*⌋+1)/120 with a*=τGlog2(2g0/(Θ+mN)). For τG=40,62,90 d this gives 0.317, 0.483 and 0.700. These are exactly the values the demo computes from its table of the network capacity, and the best of 16,384 designs in each scenario is within one day of them (a transient can overshoot the quasi steady state by a day).

9. Design ensemble, inference and identifiability

Ensemble. The design vector θ=(aE,k,KX,hE) is drawn from a scrambled Sobol sequence, log uniform on aE∈[0.004,0.25], k∈[0.2,5], KX∈[0.5,8], hE∈[30,400] d (16,384 points). The robust score is the worst case over three G6PD half lives, Prob(θ)=minτG∈{40,62,90}PτG(θ). Designs with c(t)>cmax at any time score 0.

Inference. With log toxin observations y¯j at n days, a design is accepted if

d(θ)=[1n∑j=1n(lnXj(θ)−y¯j)2]1/2≤ε=0.15,
(25)

(rejection sampling, 76 of 16,384 accepted). Adding the enzyme amount at the end of expression extends the sum by the term (lnE6(θ)−lnEobs―)2 with n+1 in the denominator. The narrowing of a parameter is reported as 1−IQR(log10θacc)/IQR(log10θprior).

Proposition 4 (structural non identifiability). Let two designs share KX and hE and satisfy aEk=aE′k′, and suppose their expression costs stay below cflat=2H(0)−mA−g0/3=1.0667. Then R, X and vDET coincide for all t, while E′=(aE′/aE)E.
Proof. Write E=aEE¯, where dE¯/dt=χh(R;KR)−δE¯ does not involve aE. Then κ=(aEk)E¯X/(Km+X), so R, X and κ depend on (aE,k) only through the product. The expression cost does depend on aE alone, but in the expression stage V(0,c)=2min{g0,H,3(2H−mA−c)}−mN=2g0−mN for every c≤cflat, so the cost does not change the delivered flux. ◻

So, from toxin data alone, logaE and logk are confounded along a line of slope −1. The accepted designs have an expression cost (5th to 95th percentile) of 0.22 to 0.89, below cflat, which is why the accepted set lies on that line (correlation of the logs −0.99). The only observation that separates them is one that depends on aE itself, the effector amount E(Texp)=aEE¯(Texp), which is why the demo adds it. The product aEk, the sensor threshold and the half life are identifiable, and so is the protected fraction, which depends on the design only through them.

12. What is exact and what is approximate

  • Exact for this toy model: Lemma 1; Proposition 1 and its marginal values; the reserve formula; the window and its closing ages; Proposition 4.
  • Exact under the quasi steady state: Proposition 2 and the bound of Proposition 3. The check in section 10 measures the error (a few percent near the transition, where the toxin level moves faster than the enzyme and the network capacity).
  • Numerical: forward Euler with Δt=0.5 h (changes the toxin level by less than 2% against Δt=0.125 h); hit and run sampling; rejection sampling for inference, which is not a full Bayesian posterior.
  • Assumptions: the network is in steady state at every instant (so no cofactor ratios); the expression stage reuses the mature cell network; the data are generated by the same model that is fitted, so identifiability results are best cases.
