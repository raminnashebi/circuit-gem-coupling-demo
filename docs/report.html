Coupling a gene circuit to a metabolic model

A toy demonstration of how a gene circuit model can be coupled to a metabolic network model, with invented numbers and simulated data.

Read this first. The cell, the circuit and every "measurement" here are made up or simulated by the script that wrote this page. The demonstration shows how the method works and what kind of answers it gives. It says nothing about real red cells, and no number here should be quoted as a biological result.

In one minute

  • The idea. Treat the gene circuit and the cell as two models that swap a few variables. The circuit tells the cell how much enzyme there is. The cell tells the circuit how much flux it can really deliver.
  • What the demonstration does. A small red cell style network, an enzyme circuit that removes a toxin using NADPH, five simulated datasets, and six steps from a one-off feasibility check to a search over 16,384 designs.
  • What came out. The network's capacity sets an upper bound on what any design can achieve. The toxin stays controlled for about 110 days, but the cell's spare redox capacity falls below the safety threshold at about 52 days. Expression strength and kcat cannot be told apart from the toxin data, yet the prediction that matters (protected life) barely changes across the designs the data allow.

1. The idea

Two things differ from a standard metabolic model. First, a mature red cell does not grow, so there is no biomass objective. The network is asked to meet maintenance tasks (pump ATP and a basal NADPH demand) and then to say how much extra the circuit can draw. Second, the circuit is made in one cell stage and acts in another, so the same coupling is run in two regimes.

2. The method in six steps

4Couple in timeEach hour the circuit ODEs propose a flux bound, the linear program returns what the cell can deliver, and the delivered flux feeds back into the toxin balance.When does the design fail, and why?

Step 5 relies on a short argument. The set of feasible DETOX fluxes is an interval from zero to the network capacity, because the feasible region is convex and zero is feasible. So the flux the cell delivers is exactly min(enzyme bound, network capacity), and the network capacity depends only on cell age and expression cost. That lets the design search use a table of exact results instead of a linear program per step. The checks in section 7 confirm that the table and the direct linear program agree.

3. The toy cell

10 metabolites and 12 reactions, in arbitrary flux units. A young cell takes up about 0.4 glucose units. Steady state means S v = 0. ATP/ADP and NADPH/NADP are conserved pairs. The pentose phosphate route (PPP) is lumped with its recycling and carbon dioxide is not tracked. Gene symbols are illustrative.

Maintenance tasks instead of growth. The pump reaction ATPASE must carry at least 0.8 and the redox reaction GSH_REDOX at least 0.12. Ageing. G6PD capacity halves every 62 days and hexokinase every 100 days. Network facts that follow. NADPH comes only from the PPP, so the network capacity for DETOX is exactly 2 × G6PD capacity − basal NADPH demand. At age 0 that is 2 × 0.4 − 0.12 = 0.68, which the linear program reproduces.

4. The circuit model

A toxin X arrives at a constant load and is also cleared slowly by other routes. The sensor reads X, a regulator R follows, and the effector enzyme E removes X using NADPH.

dR/dt = on(t) · a_R · h(X; K_X) − d_R · R                      h(u; K) = u² / (K² + u²)
dE/dt = on(t) · a_E · h(R; K_R) − d_E(t) · E
dX/dt = L − v_DETOX − k_c · X
bound:  v_DETOX ≤ kcat · E · X / (Km + X)     then the network returns what it can deliver
cost:   ATP drawn by expression = c_R · a_R · h(X; K_X) + c_E · a_E · h(R; K_R)   (expression stage only)
  • Expression stage (6 days, stands in for the erythroblast). on = 1. Making proteins costs ATP, which the network must supply. The same toy network stands in for the erythroblast. In the real project this stage needs its own model, with growth and mitochondria.
  • Mature stage (120 days). on = 0. There are no ribosomes, so no new protein: R disappears in about two days and E decays with a half-life of 150 days.
  • Targets (invented). Toxin level at most 4 (it would be 10 without the circuit). That needs a DETOX flux of at least 0.12. Protection also needs a redox reserve of at least 2.5× the basal NADPH flux.
  • True design used to make the data: expression strength aE = 0.02, kcat = 1.0, sensor threshold KX = 2.0, enzyme half-life 150 d.

6. Results

6.1 Step 2: what limits the effector

NADPH effector. The network capacity falls from 0.68 at age 0 to 0.29 at 60 days and 0.09 at 120 days, as G6PD declines. The marginal values name the limit: one more unit of G6PD capacity buys 2.0 units of detox flux, one unit less basal NADPH demand buys 1.0, and hexokinase, ATP and glucose are worth zero.

ATP effector. A different host constraint binds. The network capacity is 1.18 at age 0 and 0.05 at 120 days. Here hexokinase capacity is worth 2.0 per unit and G6PD nothing. The same pipeline tells you which cofactor limits which kind of effector.

Reading note: the dashed line is the flux needed to hold the toxin at its target. At 120 days even a perfectly expressed enzyme can no longer reach it.

6.2 Step 2: the safe operating window

The window is bounded from below by the detox needed and from above by the reserve that must be kept. It narrows as the cell ages and closes at about 58 days. After about 107 days no enzyme level reaches the needed flux at all. This is the kind of figure that tells an experimental team how strongly to express an enzyme, and in which cells it will work.

6.3 Step 3: what each dataset tells us

  • Exchange rates alone cannot fix the PPP split. Sampling the flux space gives a PPP share of glucose between 0.15 and 0.48 (95% range). Adding the tracer fraction narrows it to 0.14 to 0.21. The true value, 0.146, is inside. The feasible range of the PPP flux itself shrinks from 0.06–0.24 to 0.06–0.09.
  • Fluxes tell you what the cell does now, enzyme levels tell you what it can do under load. The network capacity for the effector depends on G6PD capacity, which exchange rates and a basal tracer fraction do not measure. At age 60 the predicted network capacity is 0.11 to 1.20 without the enzyme assay (my invented prior) and 0.255 to 0.293 with it. The true value is 0.289. The fitted G6PD half-life is 64.5 days (95% interval 58–72) against a true 62.

This is the practical reason for the "what to measure" list: in a real project, it is the enzyme activity or abundance by cell age, not another round of exchange rates, that narrows the prediction.

6.4 Step 4: coupling in time

  1. Expression stage. The sensor reads the toxin and switches the circuit on. The toxin falls from 10 to about 1.8. The ATP drawn for making proteins peaks at 0.34 flux units and the cell never has less than 0.80 spare. No crisis occurs.
  2. Maturation. With no ribosomes, the regulator falls to zero within about two days and the circuit can no longer respond. Only the enzyme pool is left and it decays slowly. This is the two regime picture from the slides.
  3. The cost to the host. The detox flux uses NADPH, so the redox reserve falls as G6PD declines. It crosses the 2.5× threshold at about 52 days. The toxin stays under its target until about 110 days, when the network itself becomes the limit (from about 106 days).

The lesson is the point of coupling: judged by the circuit output alone, the design looks successful for more than three months. Only the coupled model shows that the safety margin of the host is gone after about seven weeks.

6.5 Steps 5 and 6: designs and identifiability

  • The network caps every design. Of 16,384 designs, 13% fail because expression costs more ATP than the cell has to spare. For the rest, the best protected fraction of mature life is 0.32, 0.49 and 0.71 in the fast, baseline and slow G6PD scenarios. These equal the network's own limits (0.32, 0.48, 0.70) to within a day. Tuning the circuit cannot beat the host.
  • Expression and kcat are not identifiable separately. 76 designs reproduce the toxin time course, and they lie on a line (correlation of the logs -0.99). The data narrow the product aE × kcat by 94% but expression by only 26% and kcat by 4%. The reason is structural: in the mature cell and in the toxin balance, enzyme level only acts through kcat × E.
  • One more measurement resolves it. Adding the simulated enzyme amount at the end of expression narrows expression to 79% and kcat to 80% (from 26% and 4%), and the ATP burden during expression from 0.22–0.89 to 0.30–0.44 (true value 0.34). This burden is where expression and kcat differ: the cheaper design is the one with higher kcat and lower expression.
  • The prediction is identifiable even though the parameters are not. Across all designs consistent with the toxin data, the protected fraction of life lies between 0.43 and 0.45 (true design 0.43). This is the same separation of robust predictions from non-identifiable parameters as in the bioenergetics ensembles.

8. Limits of this demonstration

  • Invented cell. The network is a teaching toy. It leaves out NADH balance, 2,3-BPG, ion and volume dynamics, carbon dioxide and compartments. Its numbers (capacities, demands, thresholds) were chosen to be sensible, not measured.
  • Same model makes and fits the data. The identifiability results are therefore best cases. Real data add model error and noise that is not lognormal.
  • Quasi steady state. A flux balance model assumes metabolism settles much faster than the hour to day changes in the circuit. That suits fluxes, but it gives no cofactor ratios (NADPH/NADP⁺, GSH/GSSG). A real application would need a small kinetic core for those. The redox reserve here is a flux margin standing in for them.
  • Simple circuit. One toxin, one effector, no cell to cell variation, no protein burden beyond ATP, no loss of function from damage.
  • Simple inference. The design search accepts designs whose predictions fall within a tolerance of the data (76 of 16,384). This shows structure, not a full Bayesian posterior.
  • One network for two stages. The expression stage reuses the mature cell network.

9. Questions and next steps

Questions it may prompt

  • Is steady state valid at each step? For flux questions yes, since central metabolism relaxes in seconds or minutes. For cofactor ratios I would add a kinetic core of central red cell metabolism.
  • Why a linear program per step and not a kinetic model? The network is large and its kinetics are partly unknown. Constraints need no rate laws. Where dynamics matter, a kinetic model is used for the core only.
  • How would this scale? Replace the toy matrix with RBC-GEM for the mature cell, with stage extensions from Human-GEM such as mitochondrial reactions for the reticulocyte. The functions need only a stoichiometric matrix, bounds and gene rules, so the same code runs on a model loaded with COBRApy.
  • What if the real circuit is not an enzyme? Any circuit output that changes a flux bound, a demand reaction or a regulatory switch enters the same way.

Next steps for a real application

  1. Swap in a real stage specific red cell model and literature capacities (enzyme activities by cell age).
  2. Add a kinetic core for central metabolism to get NADPH/NADP⁺ and ATP ratios.
  3. Use measured metabolomics as exchange intervals, as in step 3, and test identifiability before the experiments.
  4. Give the erythroblast stage its own model with growth and mitochondria.

Glossary

Metabolic network (GEM). The list of reactions a cell can run, as a stoichiometric matrix S. Flux. Rate of a reaction. Linear programming. Optimising a linear goal under linear constraints. Network capacity. Largest feasible flux of one reaction (the maximum from FVA). Marginal value (shadow price). Gain in the goal from relaxing one constraint by one unit. FVA. Flux variability analysis, the minimum and maximum of each flux. Flux sampling. Drawing random feasible flux states without choosing an objective. G6PD, PPP. The enzyme and pathway that make NADPH from glucose. Effector. The enzyme the circuit produces. Identifiable. A parameter is identifiable if the data can fix it.
